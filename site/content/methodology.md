## What D measures

A grade belongs to one exact record, such as one contract on one network. Uniswap's four exchange cores are four
records with four grades, and UNI, the Uniswap token, is a fifth. EDI's record for Uniswap without a version has no
grade: EDI requires a version and pool scope, because one grade for the whole brand would hide the differences between
hooks and wrappers. The directory never builds a grade for a brand by combining others, and holding a governance token
is not using the protocol it governs.

A grade describes control, not size, safety, returns or use. Use appears separately, as dated figures with their own
sources. In profiles and stories every grade is a reference to EDI rather than typed text, so the prose follows EDI when
an assessment changes. When a story relies on something EDI has not reviewed, such as a pool's hook, an issuer's
allowlist or a company's loan product, it names that control in words and gives it no grade.

## What is included

Every record in the EDI registry is listed: networks, assets and protocols. A listing is not an endorsement, a security
audit or investment advice, and it does not certify that anything is safe or successful. Each record is one exact
mechanism, such as one contract on one network; the same name on another network or contract is a different object.

Priority records have edited profiles that explain what they make possible, with sourced evidence. Every other record
shows EDI's own description of what was reviewed, so nothing is invented to fill a page, with an editorial role such as
dollar token, exchange or lending where one fits. Aliases, such as the ticker a token's contract reports, help search;
they do not mean that a token with the same ticker elsewhere is the same thing.

Organizations, products and deployments outside EDI, such as an issuer, a company's loan product or a token's copy on
another network, appear as context in stories and connections. They are labelled as not assessed by EDI and never
receive a grade. Connections are editorial, typed and sourced: one party issues, settles on, provides an interface to,
integrates with, is used as collateral for, or economically references another. A connection never changes a grade;
only EDI's own dependency records do.

## What counts as success

Success means evidence of useful operation: sustained use, repeated integrations, an established economic function,
adoption by a real workflow, meaningful settlement or lending activity, or a demonstrated benefit to users. A deployed
contract alone proves neither adoption nor benefit, and market value or a famous logo is evidence of neither. Stories do
not claim that decentralization caused adoption unless separate evidence shows it.

Each story follows one outcome by one route, such as distribution to new users, programmable settlement, access to
liquidity or reusable infrastructure, and carries the label of the strongest evidence it cites for it, never a stronger
one. Independently reproduced means the directory read the figure from the chain itself, at pinned blocks, with a script
anyone can re-run. Figures a company publishes about its own product are reported adoption, and an announcement stays
announced until use appears. Where the evidence stops short, the story says so: trading of fund shares that was
announced, but did not appear in the shares' transfers on Ethereum, stays labelled as announced.

## Coverage

Coverage counts records and evidence, not importance. The directory reads Ethereum mainnet itself, and Base where a
story follows activity there. A figure that a source reports across several networks without splitting them is labelled
that way and never presented as Ethereum activity. Activity off chain, on networks the directory does not read, or in
share classes it does not follow is outside its figures unless a figure says otherwise.

Profiles and stories are written in English. Pages in other languages translate the interface and EDI's own text, and
mark the English editorial text as English.

## Dates and freshness

A balance or supply dated a given day is the state at 00:00 UTC on that day, read at the last block before midnight. A
monthly figure covers every block whose timestamp falls in that month, in UTC. A figure keeps its own date wherever it
appears; a newer page does not make it current.

Each source records when it was published, where it says, and when an editor last read it. A profile's or story's
review date says when an editor last checked its prose and sources. It does not refresh a grade, which follows EDI's
own review dates.

## Corrections

Grades are corrected in EDI itself, through its own research process. The directory shows the change as soon as EDI
publishes it.

Editorial errors, such as a wrong figure, date or source, are corrected in the directory's content, and every
correction is listed with its date. Any independently reproduced figure can be checked by re-running its read from
[the directory's evidence folder](https://github.com/ryanberckmans/ethereum-decentralization-index/tree/main/site/content/evidence):
the script reads the same pinned blocks and reports whether each result, and the fingerprint of the events behind it,
still matches. A mismatch is investigated and corrected, never silently overwritten. A source that moves or disappears
is replaced with a working one, or its claim is withdrawn.
